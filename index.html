A · Kenapa kita berinteraksiA · Why we interact at all

Interaksi adalah cara bertanyaInteraction is a way of asking

Klaim sesi iniThe claim of this session

Di display yang tidak bisa dioperasikan, mata adalah satu-satunya input device. Setiap interaksi yang ingin ditambahkan desainer adalah pertanyaan yang gagal dijawab layout sendiri, dan pertanyaan yang tak terjawab berubah jadi visual search yang harus dikerjakan pembaca.

In a display that cannot be operated, the eye is the only input device. Every interaction a designer would have added is a question the layout must now answer on its own, and every question left unanswered becomes a visual search the reader has to carry out.

Hick–Hyman: memilih memakan waktuHick–Hyman: choosing costs time

RT = a + b · log₂(C)

  • Reaksi sederhana ≈ 130 ms.
  • Tiap bit pilihan ≈ 160 ms (kondisi optimal). 8 pilihan = 3 bit ≈ +480 ms.
  • Yang diujikan bentuk kurvanya (logaritmik: tiap penggandaan pilihan menambah biaya tetap), bukan konstantanya.
  • Speed–accuracy trade-off: membolehkan sesekali salah membuat respons lebih cepat.
  • Setiap legend adalah menu. Tab, filter list, KPI tile juga choice set. Dashboard tanpa klik pun tetap punya menu complexity; pembaca memilih dengan mata.
  • Fix: label langsung di mark → C = 1 → log₂(1) = 0, pilihan hilang.
  • Simple reaction ≈ 130 ms.
  • Each bit of choice ≈ 160 ms (good conditions). 8 options = 3 bits ≈ +480 ms.
  • What is examined is the shape of the curve (logarithmic: each doubling of options adds a fixed cost), not the constants.
  • Speed–accuracy trade-off: allowing occasional mistakes makes responses faster.
  • Every legend is a menu. Tabs, filter lists and KPI tiles are choice sets too. A dashboard with nothing to click still has menu complexity; the reader chooses with the eyes.
  • Fix: put the label on the mark → C = 1 → log₂(1) = 0, the choice disappears.
Versi super gampang

Bayangkan kamu monyet di depan pohon. Kalau cuma ada 1 pisang, kamu langsung ambil, tanpa mikir. Kalau ada 2 pisang, kamu mikir sebentar, "yang mana ya?". Kalau ada 8 pisang, mikirnya lebih lama lagi.

  • RT = berapa lama kamu bengong sebelum ambil pisang.
  • C = jumlah pisang (pilihan) di depanmu.
  • a = waktu minimal, bahkan kalau pisangnya cuma satu (≈130 ms, sekedip).
  • log₂(C) = "berapa kali pisangnya harus dibagi dua sampai tinggal satu". 8 → 4 → 2 → 1 = 3 kali = 3 bit.
  • b = harga tiap kali bagi dua (≈160 ms).

Triknya: menambah pisang dua kali lipat cuma menambah satu langkah mikir. 8 ke 16 pisang cuma tambah ≈160 ms, bukan dua kali lebih lama. Itulah kenapa kurvanya melengkung landai, bukan garis lurus naik.

8 pilihan: 130 + 160 × 3 = 610 ms
1 pilihan (label langsung): 130 + 160 × 0 = 130 ms

Hubungannya ke dashboard: legend itu tumpukan pisang. Tiap kali mata mencocokkan warna bar ke legend, otak memilih dari C pilihan. Tulis label langsung di bar, pisangnya tinggal satu, mikirnya hilang.

Explained like you know nothing

Picture a monkey at a tree. With 1 banana it grabs it instantly, no thinking. With 2 bananas it pauses: "which one?". With 8 bananas it pauses longer.

  • RT = how long you stare before grabbing.
  • C = how many bananas (options) there are.
  • a = the minimum time, even with one banana (≈130 ms, a blink).
  • log₂(C) = "how many times you halve the pile until one is left". 8 → 4 → 2 → 1 = 3 times = 3 bits.
  • b = the price of each halving (≈160 ms).

The trick: doubling the bananas adds only one more step of thinking. Going from 8 to 16 adds ≈160 ms, not twice the time. That's why the curve bends and flattens instead of climbing in a straight line.

8 options: 130 + 160 × 3 = 610 ms
1 option (direct label): 130 + 160 × 0 = 130 ms

Why it matters for dashboards: a legend is a pile of bananas. Each time the eye matches a bar's colour to the legend, the brain picks among C options. Write the label on the bar and there's one banana left, so the thinking disappears.

B · Manipulation loop

Fitts: menunjuk juga memakan waktuFitts: pointing costs time

T = a + b · log₂(D/W + 1)

  • D = jarak ke target, W = lebar target. log₂(D/W+1) = index of difficulty.
  • Throughput manusia ≈ 4 bit/detik (jari, pergelangan, atau lengan).
  • Contoh Ware: gerak 16 cm ke target 0,5 cm ≈ 5 bit, lebih dari 1 detik dibanding target yang sudah di bawah kursor.
  • Loop iteratif: nilai jarak → gerak → koreksi dari feedback visual → ulangi.
  • Lag mengalikan biaya: T = a + b(HumanTime + MachineLag)·log₂(D/W+1). Paling parah pada target kecil; alasan utama Ware menyorot VR.
  • Fix view-only: taruh jawaban tepat di tempat mata mendarat pertama.
  • D = distance to the target, W = its width. log₂(D/W+1) = the index of difficulty.
  • Human throughput ≈ 4 bits/second (finger, wrist or forearm).
  • Ware's example: moving 16 cm to a 0.5 cm target ≈ 5 bits, over a second longer than a target already under the cursor.
  • An iterative loop: judge distance → move → correct from visual feedback → repeat.
  • Lag multiplies the cost: T = a + b(HumanTime + MachineLag)·log₂(D/W+1). Worst with small targets; the main reason Ware singles out VR.
  • View-only fix: put the answer where the eye lands first.

Waktu dengan lag memakai konstanta ilustratif dari halaman kuliah (b·HumanTime = 0,25 s/bit), hanya untuk menunjukkan bentuknya.Lagged times use the lecture page's placeholder constant (b·HumanTime = 0.25 s/bit), only to show the shape.

Versi super gampang

Sekarang monyetnya sudah memilih pisang, tinggal meraihnya. Dua hal bikin lama: pisangnya jauh (D besar) dan pisangnya kecil (W kecil). Pisang besar di depan hidung? Langsung dapat. Pisang kecil di ujung dahan? Tangan harus pelan-pelan, meleset, koreksi, meleset, koreksi.

  • T = lama waktu sampai tanganmu kena pisangnya.
  • D = jarak ke pisang. W = besar pisangnya.
  • D/W = "seberapa jauh dibanding seberapa besar". Jauh + kecil = angka besar = susah.
  • log₂(D/W + 1) = tingkat kesulitan (index of difficulty), dalam bit. +1 cuma supaya hasilnya tidak pernah negatif.
  • Manusia bisa "membayar" sekitar 4 bit per detik. Jadi 5 bit ≈ 1,25 detik.

Seperti Hick, ada log-nya: jarak dua kali lipat cuma tambah satu langkah kesulitan. Tapi kalau pisangnya diperkecil setengah, kesulitannya juga naik satu langkah.

16 cm ke target 0,5 cm: log₂(16/0,5 + 1) = log₂(33) ≈ 5 bit ≈ 1,25 s
16 cm ke target 4 cm: log₂(16/4 + 1) = log₂(5) ≈ 2,3 bit ≈ 0,6 s

Lag: bayangkan tiap kali monyet menggerakkan tangan, matanya baru melihat hasilnya 0,3 detik kemudian. Dia terus kelewatan dan harus balik. Makin kecil pisangnya, makin banyak koreksi, jadi lag-nya dibayar berkali-kali. Itulah kenapa rumusnya mengalikan lag dengan tingkat kesulitan, dan kenapa VR yang ngelag bikin pusing.

Hubungannya ke dashboard: dashboard view-only tidak punya tangan, jadi "pisangnya" harus sudah ada tepat di tempat mata jatuh pertama kali. Jarak nol = waktu nol.

Explained like you know nothing

Now the monkey has picked a banana and only has to reach it. Two things slow it down: the banana is far (big D) and the banana is small (small W). A big banana under your nose? Instant. A tiny banana at the end of a branch? The hand goes slowly: miss, correct, miss, correct.

  • T = time until your hand touches the banana.
  • D = distance to the banana. W = how big it is.
  • D/W = "how far compared to how big". Far + small = big number = hard.
  • log₂(D/W + 1) = the index of difficulty, in bits. The +1 just keeps it from going negative.
  • Humans "pay" about 4 bits per second. So 5 bits ≈ 1.25 seconds.

Like Hick, there's a log: doubling the distance adds only one step of difficulty. But halving the banana's size also adds a step.

16 cm to a 0.5 cm target: log₂(16/0.5 + 1) = log₂(33) ≈ 5 bits ≈ 1.25 s
16 cm to a 4 cm target: log₂(16/4 + 1) = log₂(5) ≈ 2.3 bits ≈ 0.6 s

Lag: imagine that every time the monkey moves its hand, its eyes only see the result 0.3 s later. It keeps overshooting and coming back. The smaller the banana, the more corrections, so the lag is paid again and again. That's why the formula multiplies lag by the difficulty, and why laggy VR makes people sick.

Why it matters for dashboards: a view-only dashboard has no hand, so the "banana" must already sit where the eye lands first. Zero distance = zero time.

Power law of practice

log(Tₙ) = C − α·log(n). Novice cepat membaik, expert butuh ribuan percobaan untuk 10%. Pembaca dashboard view-only selalu novice di penglihatan pertama.log(Tₙ) = C − α·log(n). A novice improves fast; an expert needs thousands of trials for another 10%. A view-only dashboard's reader is always a novice on first viewing.

Tiga "law" kecil lainnya, versi gampang
  • Steering law (Accot & Zhai), v = W/τ: monyet jalan di atas dahan. Dahan lebar, dia bisa lari. Dahan setipis tali, dia harus merayap pelan. Kecepatan sebanding dengan lebar jalurnya. Contoh nyata: menyusuri submenu yang sempit dengan mouse.
  • Dua tangan (Guiard): satu tangan memegang dahan supaya diam (tangan non-dominan = frame), tangan lain mengupas pisang (tangan dominan = detail). Seperti memegang kertas dengan tangan kiri sambil menulis dengan tangan kanan.
  • Power law of practice, log(Tₙ) = C − α·log(n): pertama kali memanjat pohon, monyet lambat sekali. Percobaan ke-10 sudah jauh lebih cepat. Tapi setelah ke-1000, tambah cepatnya tinggal sedikit sekali. Makin jago, makin susah tambah jago. Pembaca dashboard cuma lihat sekali, jadi dia selalu monyet yang baru pertama kali memanjat.
  • Stimulus–response compatibility: kalau tangan digerakkan ke kanan, benda di layar juga harus ke kanan. Kalau arahnya dibalik, monyetnya bingung berminggu-minggu.
Three smaller "laws", made simple
  • Steering law (Accot & Zhai), v = W/τ: a monkey walking along a branch. Wide branch, it can run. Branch as thin as a rope, it has to creep. Speed is proportional to the path's width. Real example: tracing a narrow submenu with a mouse.
  • Two hands (Guiard): one hand holds the branch still (non-dominant hand = the frame), the other peels the banana (dominant hand = the detail). Like holding paper with your left hand while writing with your right.
  • Power law of practice, log(Tₙ) = C − α·log(n): the first climb is very slow. By the 10th it's much faster. After the 1000th, the extra speed-up is tiny. The better you get, the harder it is to get better. A dashboard reader looks only once, so they're always the monkey on its first climb.
  • Stimulus–response compatibility: move your hand right and the thing on screen should move right. Reverse the direction and the monkey is confused for weeks.
